What We're Building

ClearOS is an operational platform for freight-forwarding companies — specifically built for the Tanzania market. It eliminates the friction of managing imports and exports through WhatsApp groups, email attachments, and spreadsheets.

The system has two faces: an Operations Board for officers and managers, and a Customer Portal for importers to track their shipments, upload documents, and communicate with their assigned officer.

📱 Ops Board (Officers)

Desktop-first internal command centre. Officers see all active cases grouped by customer, triage urgent actions, update statuses and communicate — all in one view. No more switching between WhatsApp, email and spreadsheets.

📦 Customer Portal (Importers)

Mobile-first interface. Customers see exactly where their shipments are, what documents are needed, what it'll cost, and can message their officer directly. Designed for use on a phone in a warehouse or port.

Why This Matters

1
Reduce missed actions
Document expiry reminders, permit requests, and payment nudges are automated — officers no longer chase customers manually. The system flags when a case is blocked and who needs to act.
2
Customer trust via transparency
Customers no longer need to chase their officer for updates. The portal shows a live journey bar, document checklist, and invoice breakdown at all times. Repeat business increases with visibility.
3
Operational leverage
One officer can manage 40+ active cases simultaneously. The risk-scoring engine auto-surfaces what needs attention. Managers see revenue pipeline, aging invoices, and team performance from one dashboard.
4
Multi-tenant from day one
The architecture supports multiple freight companies on a single codebase. Each company gets their own data silo, custom branding, and user accounts. SaaS pricing unlocks once V1 is proven.
02 — Problem
What's broken today
The current way freight-forwarding companies manage customer communication in Tanzania creates four critical failure points.
Fragmented communication
Critical

Customer updates are scattered across WhatsApp, email, and phone. Officers cannot see a unified thread per shipment. Critical messages get buried. Customers receive inconsistent responses depending on which officer they reach.

No customer visibility
Critical

Customers have zero insight into their shipment status unless they call or WhatsApp their officer. This creates high inbound call volume, officer frustration, and customer anxiety — especially when shipments are delayed at customs.

Manual document tracking
High

Documents such as Bills of Lading, TMDA permits, and C36 forms are tracked via spreadsheets or memory. When a required document is missing, officers may not know until customs flags it — costing storage fees and delays.

Revenue leakage
High

Invoices are generated manually and late. Storage charges are not tracked systematically. Some shipments are delivered without final invoicing. Finance has no real-time view of outstanding receivables or billing status per case.

03 — Users
Who uses ClearOS
Five distinct roles, two portals. System-level access controls enforce what each role sees and can do.

Internal Users — Operations Board

AD
Admin / Owner
Full system access
Configures the platform: creates user accounts, sets workflow rules, manages billing. Sees all customers and all cases. Has override ability on any case. Manages multi-tenant settings if applicable.
MG
Manager
Read + supervise; no case editing
Reviews analytics dashboard, monitors team performance, approves high-value invoices, and escalates issues. Does not handle individual shipment cases but sees all of them. Primary consumer of reporting.
OF
Operations Officer
Full case management on assigned cases
Primary daily user. Updates shipment statuses, communicates with customers, uploads/requests documents, processes customs entries, and triggers notifications. May be assigned a portfolio of 20–50 active customers.
FN
Finance Officer
Invoice & payments only
Generates and finalises invoices, records payments, manages outstanding balances, and runs finance reports. No operational case management. Sees costs and revenue across all cases but cannot change statuses.

External Users — Customer Portal

CU
Customer / Importer
Self-serve portal access (mobile-first)
Business owner or procurement manager at an importing company. Uses the portal on a phone — often from a port, warehouse, or office. Needs to: check shipment progress, upload missing documents, see upcoming invoices, and message their officer. Comfort with technology varies; UI must be very simple.

06 — Workflows
Core workflows
The platform orchestrates three main workflows: the shipment clearance lifecycle, the document collection loop, and the invoice-to-payment cycle.

1. Shipment Clearance Lifecycle

Every import case moves through 6 milestone stages. Each stage transition triggers notifications to the customer and updates the journey bar in the customer portal.

Status codes within each stage

Each milestone can hold one of several sub-statuses (e.g. "Customs lodged" → "Awaiting permit" or "Under examination"). Officers update status from the detail panel. Each change logs an entry to the update feed and may trigger a customer notification.

2. Document Collection Loop

1
Case created → document checklist generated
System knows which documents are required based on shipment type (sea / air / land) and goods category (general, regulated, controlled). A checklist is pre-populated at case creation.
2
Officer flags missing documents
As documents arrive, the officer marks them received. Any document not received by a configurable deadline triggers an automatic reminder to the customer via WhatsApp/email.
3
Customer uploads via portal
The "Action Needed" banner in the customer portal surfaces exactly which documents are missing with a one-tap upload button. Files are stored in MinIO (S3-compatible), secured per tenant.
4
Officer receives upload notification
On successful upload, the officer gets a real-time notification on the ops board. The document row updates automatically. If a TMDA or other regulatory permit, the system flags it for officer verification before marking it valid.

3. Invoice-to-Payment Cycle

1
Estimated invoice shown early
As soon as a case is opened, an estimated cost breakdown is visible to the customer. Line items include: clearance service, transport, agency & handling, and placeholder for storage (TBD). This sets expectations and reduces billing disputes.
2
Finance officer finalises invoice at delivery
Once the shipment is delivered, Finance finalises the invoice — adding storage charges and confirming transport costs. A PDF is generated and sent to the customer via email. Government duties (TRA) are shown separately.
3
Payment recorded; case closed
Finance records the payment (bank transfer, M-Pesa, etc.). The case moves to "Closed". Outstanding invoices appear in the manager's aging receivables dashboard.

11 — Delivery
Roadmap & phases
V1 targets 8 weeks for a live system with one pilot customer. V2 adds intelligence and scale. V3 opens the platform.

Phase 1 — Foundation (Weeks 1–3)

Week 1
Project setup & data model
Monorepo scaffolding, Docker Compose stack, PostgreSQL schema with all core tables, migrations, seed data. Auth system with RBAC. Basic Fastify routes for cases and customers.
5 days
Week 2
Ops board — case list & detail panel
React ops board with left nav, KPI bar, customer-grouped case list, status filter chips, and slide-in detail panel. Timeline tab functional. Status updates save to DB.
5 days
Week 3
Customer portal — shipment list & detail sheet
Full mobile customer portal. Shipment cards, segment tabs, journey bar, update feed, document checklist with upload, estimated invoice view. PWA manifest and offline shell.
5 days

Phase 2 — Communications & Finance (Weeks 4–6)

Week 4
WhatsApp & email integration
Meta Cloud API webhook setup. Inbound message routing to case update feed. Officer can reply via WhatsApp from ops board. Email send for formal notifications. BullMQ notification queue.
5 days
Week 5
Document management & MinIO storage
MinIO setup, per-tenant bucket policies. Upload endpoint with virus scan hook. Signed download URLs with expiry. Document verification flow for regulated permits. Missing-doc reminder job.
5 days
Week 6
Invoice builder & Finance portal
Finance role view. Invoice line-item builder. PDF generation (Puppeteer). Payment recording. Outstanding receivables list. Government duty separation. Invoice send to customer via email.
5 days

Phase 3 — Polish & Pilot (Weeks 7–8)

Week 7
Real-time, notifications & risk scoring
WebSocket integration for live ops board updates. Push notification plumbing for customer portal. Basic risk-score computation: docs overdue, no update 48h, customs hold. Ops board risk flag column.
5 days
Week 8
Pilot onboarding, QA & hardening
Onboard first pilot customer. Load test. Security audit (input validation, IDOR checks, rate limiting). Fix pilot feedback. Deploy to production VPS. Hand over to operations team with runbook.
5 days

V2 — Intelligence Layer (Months 3–5)

Advanced analytics

Manager dashboard with case throughput, officer performance, revenue trends, duty forecasting.

TRA duty calculator

Auto-estimate import duty from HS code + CIF value using TRA tariff schedule. Show customer before customs.

Risks & mitigations
Key risks identified across technical, operational, and market dimensions. Each has a specific mitigation plan.
RiskLikelihoodImpactMitigation
WhatsApp API approval delay
Meta takes 1–4 weeks to approve business accounts		 Medium High Apply for Meta Business verification in week 1 in parallel. Build email-only fallback. WhatsApp feature is additive; core workflow works without it.
Customer adoption friction
Some customers prefer WhatsApp calls; portal may feel unfamiliar		 Medium Medium Pilot with 1–2 tech-comfortable customers first. Officer can use portal on their behalf. WhatsApp integration means customers still get updates in WhatsApp even if they never open the portal.
Scope creep in V1
Stakeholders request features beyond 8-week scope		 High Medium Feature matrix (see Section 05) is the contract. New requests go to V2 backlog. Weekly demo keeps stakeholders engaged without derailing delivery.
Network reliability in Tanzania
Intermittent 3G/4G; customer portal must work offline		 Medium Medium PWA service worker caches the shell and last-fetched shipment data. Offline state shown clearly. No actions that silently fail — queue locally and sync when reconnected.
Data security & GDPR/PDPA
Customs data is sensitive; multi-tenant data leakage risk		 Low High PostgreSQL row-level security per tenant. JWT never crosses tenants. MinIO per-tenant buckets. Week 8 includes a formal security audit checklist: IDOR, input validation, rate limiting, signed URLs.
Single-server infrastructure
V1 single VPS is a single point of failure		 Low Medium Daily automated DB backups to separate location. Docker Compose with named volumes. Recovery runbook in repo. V2 moves to managed Postgres + object storage for resilience.
