What it does

A clearing agent's whole back office, in one login.

Hudumika grew from a customs-clearance system (ClearOS) into a full operating layer for the businesses around it — the same tenant that lodges a TANSAD declaration also runs its payroll, its helpdesk and its sales pipeline through the same platform, with one identity provider and one set of books underneath every app.

What it captures

Real operational records, not form submissions.

Every module writes into the same tenant-scoped Postgres schema. A sample of what actually gets stored, by domain:

Customs & freight

  • DeclarationsTANSAD numbers, HS codes, duty/VAT assessment, lane and release status
  • Origin & riskEAC/AfCFTA rules-of-origin certificates, dangerous-goods class, sanctions-screening hits
  • ShipmentsAWB/BL number, container number, vessel/voyage, ETA/ETD, stage history
  • Depot & releaseEquipment interchange receipts, release orders, delivery notes

Finance

  • LedgerJournal entries, trial balance, GL period close
  • AR / APSales invoices, supplier bills, credit notes, recurring invoices
  • Assets & budgetsFixed-asset register with depreciation, department budgets vs. actuals
  • CashBank reconciliation lines, FX rates, petty-cash deposit/disbursement approvals

People

  • AttendanceBiometric-device clock-in/out timestamps, live clocked-in status
  • WorkforceShift rosters, leave requests and balances, payroll runs
  • RecruitmentPipeline stage per candidate, interview notes
  • IdentityPasskey/2FA enrollment, government-ID verification level, session devices

Customer engagement

  • SupportTickets and every message, across WhatsApp, email, SMS and in-app channels
  • Calls & meetingsDuration, participants, host controls, AI meeting summaries
  • SalesLeads, deals, quotations, per-customer agreed contract pricing
  • CommsSMS sends and delivery receipts, opt-outs, calendar bookings

This is an internal engineering read of build/audit history — not a test-coverage report or a formally computed metric. Each app below is tiered from what has actually shipped, been RLS-audited and verified against live data during development, versus what still runs on partial data paths or an early feature set. Figures move as a standing production-readiness audit (HUD-0023 onward) traces each app's real golden-path workflows live against the database, not just re-reads the code. Latest pass: 29 golden-path journeys traced live end-to-end against Postgres — 12 real bugs found and fixed (including one CRITICAL cross-customer authorization bypass) — which is what moved FinOps, CRM, NexusHR, ComplyOS and Tracking below, uncovered that Projects' enterprise portfolio/procurement layer doesn't work at all, confirmed FinOps's GL period close correctly folds a real corporate-income-tax accrual into year-end retained earnings, and corrected Store's own description below — its real backend is an internal software marketplace, not a physical-goods one. Email moved up separately after a dedicated pass closed its remaining named gaps — real Message-ID/References threading with a merged cross-folder conversation view, scheduled send with a Gmail-style undo window, multiple attachments, Postgres full-text search, user-defined labels, canned-reply templates, bulk actions beyond delete, and a live IMAP connection test — each verified end-to-end against the running API. Read-receipt confirmation keeps one inherent ceiling no implementation can close (most mail clients block remote images by default), so it's surfaced in the UI as best-effort rather than claimed as reliable. NexusHR moved up similarly after its own two-part pass: a live-traced sweep of the 10 newer sections added since the original 8-milestone build (Workforce Planning, Training, Surveys, Case Management, Checklists, Benefits, Groups, Org Chart, Employment Records, Documents, Visitors) found and fixed 7 real issues — several cross-tenant id-smuggling gaps where a caller-supplied id from another tenant would otherwise attach, a couple of endpoints missing their RBAC gate entirely, and a recurring bug where CUSTOMER-portal accounts leaked into internal staff views — plus a standing authorization gap on stopping/acknowledging someone else's clock session, and a genuine new capability: Org Chart is now actually consulted to resolve a person's real manager, not just drawn as a standalone diagram.

Architecture

One tenant, one wall.

🔒

Every tenant's data sits behind genuine Postgres Row-Level Security — the API connects as a restricted, non-superuser role (hudumika_app) with every RLS-enabled table under FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY, so a query missing its tenant scope is rejected by the database itself, not just filtered by application code. That's the floor; every route still scopes its own queries explicitly on top of it.
